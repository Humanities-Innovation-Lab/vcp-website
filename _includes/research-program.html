Research Program

HDI uses a multi-method research approach combining ethnographic interviews and participant observation, comparative case analysis, systematic mapping reviews of the literature, historical research, and collaborative engagement with researchers and information professionals.

The project examines how humanities scholars work across diverse disciplinary, institutional, and cultural heritage contexts, and how their practices shape — and are shaped by — the design, governance, and long-term stewardship of research and cultural heritage infrastructure. Rather than asking how researchers should adapt to existing infrastructure, HDI turns the question around, beginning from the premise that the humanities already have robust, if often intuitive, traditions for producing, preserving, and circulating the materials information professionals call “data” — and asking how infrastructure can be built to support them.

The research unfolds across three connected strands: a historical strand tracing how editions, transcriptions, facsimiles, and other representations of cultural objects have long been collected and shared — an early, intuitive form of data infrastructure; an ethnographic strand documenting how scholars across fields like literary studies, art history, archaeology, classics, and history work with their materials; and a comparative strand examining how digital collections are designed through collaboration between researchers and infrastructure professionals.

This work contributes to scholarly dialogue on

  • Humanities research practices and data cultures
  • Digital cultural heritage infrastructure
  • Governance, stewardship, and sustainability of digital collections
  • Research data management and policy
  • Cultural data ethics and community authority
  • The broader implications of the digital transformation of the human cultural record
